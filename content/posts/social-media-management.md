Every business feels like it should be "posting more." But more posts isn't the goal — being remembered by the right people is. So here's an honest look at what social media actually does for a business, and what's worth paying for.

## What you're actually paying for

"Managing social media" sounds like one thing. It's really a few different jobs stitched together, and the value comes from doing them consistently:

- **Posts** — the steady presence that keeps you from looking dead when someone checks your page
- **Reels** — the part that actually reaches new people who don't follow you yet
- **Editing** — what makes a phone clip look intentional instead of random
- **Brand** — the look, tone, and feel that makes your page recognisable in a scroll

Any one of these on its own does very little. Together, they turn a page nobody visits into one that quietly does your selling for you.

## The honest truth about reels

Reels get reach, but reach isn't the point — the right reach is. A viral video that brings zero customers is a nice ego boost and nothing more.

Good reels aren't about trends or dancing. They're about showing what you do, who you help, and why you're worth trusting — in a few seconds, before someone scrolls past. That takes an idea, a clean edit, and a reason for the viewer to care. That's the actual work.

## Why consistency beats effort

Most businesses post hard for two weeks, get tired, and stop. Then they wonder why nothing happened.

Social media rewards showing up steadily far more than showing up big. A calm, consistent page that posts every week beats a page that posts ten times and then goes quiet for two months. This is exactly the part that's hard to do yourself once real work picks up — and the part an agency is genuinely worth it for.

## How to not waste money

The biggest waste isn't posting too little — it's posting without a point. Random posts with no plan, no look, and no message just fill a feed. They don't build anything.

Start with a clear brand and a simple, steady rhythm. Add more only when there's a reason to. You don't need to be everywhere; you need to be good in one place.
